# AG Refining

## Register

brand

The site is the sales channel. There is no app behind it. Design carries the
credibility that a stranger needs before they let a truck come to their facility
and drive away with valuable metal.

## Product purpose

AG Refining buys silver-bearing material from Houston-area businesses and
individuals, and pays for it. The website exists to turn a search result into a
scheduled pickup.

Every page answers one question: what do you have, and how does it become cash.

The single conversion action is **Schedule a Free Pickup**. The secondary action
is a phone call. Nothing else on the site competes with those two.

## Users

**Primary, commercial.** Facility managers, office managers, lab supervisors,
and owner-operators at Houston businesses that accumulate silver as a byproduct:
hospitals and imaging centres, dental practices and labs, jewelry stores and
bench jewelers, oil and gas NDT programs, electronics and aerospace
manufacturers, universities and research labs, metal finishers.

They are not precious-metals experts. They have a storage room with boxes in it
and a vague sense the contents are worth something. They are risk-averse about
handing over material and want to know exactly what happens before it moves.

**Secondary, individual.** Estate executors, collectors, and investors selling
coins, bars, or inherited sterling flatware. Higher emotional stakes, smaller
lots, more need for reassurance and no pressure.

**Device.** Mobile first, iOS specifically. The client named iPhone users as the
main audience. Assume a phone in a warehouse or an office, one-handed, possibly
in poor light. The bottom action dock and the safe-area handling are not
decoration.

## Voice and tone

Plain, direct, unhurried. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. The reader is a
competent professional who is busy, not a mark to be closed.

Say what happens next, in order. "You see the weight, hear the offer, and choose
what happens next" is the register.

Never manufacture urgency. Never imply the reader is losing money by waiting.
The client's own copy says "no pressure to sell" and means it.

## Claim discipline

This is a regulated-adjacent business handling other people's property. The
following must not be strengthened beyond what the client has approved in
writing: insurance, assay method, fees, exact payment timing, compliance,
licenses, minimums, and shipping.

Approved qualified forms, taken from the client's own documents:

- "Free pickup for qualifying commercial and industrial accounts"
- "Immediate payment is available for most qualifying transactions"
- "Same-day service when scheduling permits"
- "On-site weighing" for qualifying pickups

Write the qualifier. It is not weasel wording, it is the difference between a
promise the business can keep and one it cannot.

## Strategic principles

1. **Start with the material.** Visitors self-identify by what is in the room,
   not by industry vertical. The materials taxonomy is the primary navigation.
2. **Show the process before asking for the lot.** Weighing, offer, payment, in
   that order, on every material page.
3. **The photograph is evidence.** Real material beats staged stock. Client
   photography outranks generated imagery wherever it exists.
4. **One idea per section.** Long pages are fine; crowded sections are not.

## Anti-references

**A dark upper half.** The client reviewed the first mockup and gave exactly one
piece of visual feedback: "the first half of the site was too dark." Heroes,
page intros, and answer bands stay light. Deep bands live below roughly the 40%
mark. This outranks any instinct toward a dramatic dark hero.

**Cash-for-gold pawn aesthetic.** No garish yellow-gold, no starburst badges, no
"WE PAY MORE!!" energy, no countdown urgency. The competitors look like this and
it is why a hospital procurement officer will not call them.

**Recycling green.** The obvious category reflex for a metals-recovery business.
Avoid entirely.

**Corporate stock photography.** No handshakes, no headsets, no diverse-team-
around-a-laptop. If a person appears, it is the Stevens family or nobody.

**Crypto and bullion-hype visual language.** No neon, no dark-mode-with-gold-
glow, no rising chart motifs.

## Brand

Family business. John Stevens built the reputation; his son Dennis runs it now.
The family story is a real asset and appears on the homepage and the about page,
told plainly and without sentimentality.

Houston-based and proud of it. The service area list is a trust signal, not SEO
filler: it tells a reader in Baytown that the truck actually comes to Baytown.

The wordmark is "AG Refining", where Ag is the chemical symbol for silver,
atomic number 47. That fact is the brand's one piece of wit and it is used
sparingly.
